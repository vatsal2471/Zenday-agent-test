§ 01

Letter from the founder.

p. 02 · signed
Zenday Inc. · Office of the Founder Toronto · April 12, 2026
Dear Managing Partner,

Accounting firms do not have a software problem. They have a labour problem — and it has been, broadly, the same problem for forty years. Every billable hour is sold at advisory rates and delivered, mostly, at data-entry speed. You already know this. Your partners have known it since the general ledger was printed on green paper.

The previous wave of automation — OCR, rule engines, templated bookkeeping — accelerated the easy sixty per cent and did nothing with the other forty. It could categorise a DoorDash and then freeze on a fifteen-thousand-dollar wire that didn't match an open invoice. Those are not edge cases. They are the reason accounting is still a human job.

Zenday is the first accountant your firm can actually assign. It handles the easy sixty per cent in under thirty seconds. It handles the ambiguous thirty-eight per cent by drafting a reply to the client, or by learning from a single correction and never asking again. What remains is the queue: about six minutes of judgment a day, pushed to your senior accountants, with the agent's reasoning already on the page. You stay the accountant of record. We do the data entry.

I would like to show you the product. If the thesis holds, you will save a senior hire's worth of time per twenty clients — and your people will finally get to do the work you hired them for.

Respectfully, Jordan A.
Jordan Alani · Founder & CEO, Zenday Ex-Intuit · Ex-Anthropic
§ 02

Executive summary.

p. 03 · three clauses
2.01 · The Product

A continuous agent, assigned per client.

Zenday is not a feature inside a ledger. It is an autonomous agent your firm assigns — one per client — with persistent memory, a toolbelt of ledger/bank/email actions, and a playbook you configure. It runs continuously, not in overnight batches.

2.02 · The Work

The full engagement, not a categoriser.

Bank feed landing, chart-of-accounts coding, reconciliation, drafted client questions, month-end P&L and variance narrative — all of it. Everything a junior bookkeeper would do, traced to the exact reasoning, confidence score, and model version for every entry.

2.03 · The Economics

Fifteen dollars. Per client. Per month.

No seat fees. No transaction caps. No setup cost. For a forty-client firm, that is $184,800 of annual savings against an offshore stack — with the senior accountants' time returned to advisory, where it was always meant to go.

§ 03

How the agent works.

p. 06 · four clauses

Each client is assigned a dedicated agent with persistent memory and an audited toolbelt. The loop below runs continuously, not on a batch schedule, and every action is logged with reasoning, confidence, cost, and model version.

3.01 · Perception

The agent reads events.

Subscribes to the bank feed, client email inbox, uploaded documents, QBO/Xero sync, and scheduled triggers. Wakes on every new event — there is no polling delay.

bank.txn.synced · $480.00 · Ramp · clear
3.02 · Reasoning

The agent decides.

Claude checks the chart of accounts, per-client learned rules, eighteen months of vendor history, and your firm's playbook. It outputs a category with a confidence score and a written rationale.

match → Office Supplies · 97% · rule:ramp→office
3.03 · Action

The agent posts.

Writes to QuickBooks or Xero through an audited, reversible ledger adapter. Every write carries reasoning, confidence, model version, source event, and cost in tokens — a full trail.

ledger.post · JE-2264 · ✓ · 0.004¢
3.04 · Learning

The agent improves.

When you override a decision, the correction becomes a permanent per-client rule. The agent does not ask the same question twice. Override rate drops under two per cent by month three.

rule.upsert · vendor → account · persisted

Sch. C

Interrogatories & answers.

p. 13 · seven Q&A

The questions firm owners actually ask on the first call. If yours is not below, write to us — the answer is either already in the product, or it is a clear no.

Q. 01
Is Zenday a bookkeeping service or software?
Software. Zenday is the agent your firm assigns to each client. You stay the accountant of record. The agent does the coding, reconciliation, and close — your team reviews the exceptions.
Q. 02
How is this different from QBO's built-in AI?
QBO suggests a category on transactions you review one-by-one. Zenday is an autonomous agent that runs continuously, learns per client, drafts client questions, and closes the month without a human driving.
Q. 03
What happens when it gets something wrong?
Low-confidence items go to the review queue instead of being posted. When you correct one, the rule becomes permanent for that client. Override rate drops below 2% by month three.
Q. 04
Which ledgers and banks do you support?
QuickBooks Online, Xero, and any bank that supports Plaid or direct feed (12,000+ US and Canadian institutions). Sage and NetSuite on the 2026 roadmap.
Q. 05
Who can see what the agent is doing?
Your team. Every entry is traceable to the exact event, reasoning, and model version. The client sees only what you choose to share.
Q. 06
What about data security?
SOC 2 Type II in progress. Customer data is not used for model training. US-hosted. BAA available on Enterprise.
Q. 07
Can I run it on one client before committing?
Yes. First client is free for thirty days. No credit card. No onboarding call — connect QBO and a bank feed, pick a client, assign the agent.
§ 05

Why we built this.

p. 14 · manifesto

We grew up inside firms. We watched senior accountants — people who could price a business, negotiate with the IRS, turn around a failing book — spend two-thirds of their week doing data entry and chasing receipts. That is not leverage. That is the opposite of leverage. Every tool built for the profession in the last twenty years has accepted this arrangement as a fact of life.

Zenday does not. The agent is assigned to the client, not installed in the ledger. It does the work the junior bookkeeper was doing, at thirty-second latency, with a full audit trail and a memory that only grows. It is not a feature. It is a headcount decision — one you make per client, for fifteen dollars a month.

We are not trying to replace your staff. We are trying to give your staff back the ten hours a week they currently spend coding Adobe subscriptions, so they can do the work they were trained for. That is the entire pitch. You assign it. It works. You review judgment. Everything else in this prospectus — the spec sheet, the tables, the exhibits — is in service of that one sentence.

— The Zenday team

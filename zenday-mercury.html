§ 01 · THESISWhy this existsREAD 2 MIN

Accounting firms don't have a software problem. They have a labor problem — and it's been the same problem for forty years.

Every billable hour in a firm is sold at advisory rates and delivered, mostly, at data-entry speed. Firms know this. Partners have known it since the general ledger was a stack of green paper. The reason the problem has not been solved is not a lack of intent. It is that, until very recently, nothing could do the work other than a human.

The previous wave — OCR, rule engines, templated bookkeeping — could accelerate the easy 60% and did nothing with the other 40%. Categorize a DoorDash; stumble on a new vendor. Post a Stripe payout; freeze on a $15K wire that doesn't match an open invoice. Those are not edge cases. They are the reason accounting is still a human job. The middle tier of judgment is where firms bleed margin, and it is exactly where legacy AI stops.

Zenday is the first accountant your firm can actually assign. It does the easy 60% in under 30 seconds. It handles the ambiguous 38% by drafting a reply to the client, or by learning from one correction and never asking again. The remaining 2% is the queue: six minutes a day of judgment, pushed to your senior accountants, with the agent's reasoning in front of them.

Straight
answers.

The questions firm owners actually ask on the first call. If yours isn't here, write to us — the answer is either in the product or it's a clear no.
Is Zenday a bookkeeping service or software?
Software. Zenday is the agent your firm assigns to each client. You stay the accountant of record. The agent does the coding, reconciliation, and close — your team reviews the exceptions.
How is this different from QBO's built-in AI?
QBO suggests a category on transactions you review one-by-one. Zenday is an autonomous agent that runs continuously, learns per client, drafts client questions, and closes the month without a human driving.
What happens when it gets something wrong?
Low-confidence items go to the review queue instead of being posted. When you correct one, the rule becomes permanent for that client. Override rate drops below 2% by month 3.
Which ledgers and banks do you support?
QuickBooks Online, Xero, and any bank that supports Plaid or direct feed (12,000+ US and Canadian institutions). Sage and NetSuite on the 2026 roadmap.
Who can see what the agent is doing?
Your team. Every entry is traceable to the exact event, reasoning, and model version. The client sees only what you choose to share.
What about data security?
SOC 2 Type II in progress. Customer data is not used for model training. US-hosted. BAA available on Enterprise.
Can I run it on one client before committing?
Yes. First client is free for 30 days. No credit card. No onboarding call — connect QBO + bank feed, pick a client, assign the agent.
§ 05 · The manifesto · Why we built this

Firms don't need
another categorizer.
They need an accountant.

We grew up inside firms. We watched senior accountants — people who could price a business, negotiate with the IRS, turn around a failing book — spend two-thirds of their week doing data entry and chasing receipts. That is not leverage. That is the opposite of leverage. And every tool built for the profession in the last twenty years has accepted this arrangement as a fact of life.

Zenday does not. The agent is assigned to the client, not installed in the ledger. It does the work the junior bookkeeper was doing, at thirty-second latency, with a full audit trail and a memory that only grows. It is not a feature. It is a headcount decision — one you make per client, for fifteen dollars a month.

We are not trying to replace your staff. We are trying to give your staff back the ten hours a week they currently spend coding Adobe subscriptions, so they can do the work they were trained for. That is the entire pitch. You assign it. It works. You review judgment. Everything else we built — the spec sheet, the tables, the comparisons — is in service of that one sentence.
